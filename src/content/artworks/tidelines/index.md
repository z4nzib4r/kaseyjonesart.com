Tidelines was a month-long participatory work on a public waterfront. Each morning, volunteers chalked the previous night's high-tide line along half a mile of seawall and walkway, dated it, and left it to weather.

![Volunteers chalking the morning line](./01.jpg)

As the month accumulated, the lines formed a readable archive of the water's reach — some days a hand's width apart, after storms a full stride. Passersby began adding their own annotations: dates they remembered, heights they feared.

![Three weeks of accumulated tidelines](./02.jpg)

The chalk was gone within weeks of the closing. The work survives as photographs, tide tables, and the habit some neighbors reported of still checking the wall each morning.
